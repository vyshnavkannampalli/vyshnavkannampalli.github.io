My Research Journey Across Three Countries

Created on August 16, 2026

2026   ·   research   semiconductors   ALD   career   ·   research

/assets/img/Research_path.png

When I first stepped into a chemistry laboratory as an undergraduate student in India, I never imagined that my journey would eventually lead me to some of Europe’s most advanced research environments. Today, as an R&D Engineer at imec in Belgium, I work on semiconductor technologies and advanced materials, but the path here has been anything but straightforward.

This is the story of curiosity, persistence, and the unexpected opportunities that science can create.

Growing Up in Kerala

I was born and raised in Kerala, India, a place known for its rich culture, education, and natural beauty. Like many students passionate about science, I was fascinated by understanding how the world works at the most fundamental level.

That curiosity eventually led me to pursue a BS-MS Dual Degree in Chemistry at the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Bhopal. During these years, I was exposed to a broad scientific foundation, ranging from chemistry and physics to materials science.

What started as an interest in chemistry slowly evolved into a fascination with materials and nanotechnology. I became increasingly interested in how we can design materials at the atomic scale to solve real-world problems, particularly in energy sectors.

Discovering Research

One of the defining moments of my academic journey came during my research projects and internships. Working in laboratories taught me that science is not simply about finding answers. It is about learning how to ask the right questions (still I am learning in my day to day life!!).

My master’s research focused on titanium dioxide nanomaterials and their photocatalytic applications. It was my first taste of independent research, where experiments often failed, results were unpredictable, and patience became as important as knowledge - and my main motive was to understand the key concept of this research and how can we get better understandings and information about this system through different characterization techniques.

These experiences convinced me that I wanted to pursue research as a career.

Moving to France for a PhD

In 2021, I moved to Marseille, France, to begin my PhD at Aix-Marseille University and the Centre Interdisciplinaire de Nanoscience de Marseille (CINaM).

Moving to a new country was exciting, but it was also challenging.

There was a new language, a different culture, unfamiliar administrative systems, and of course, the pressure of starting a doctoral program. Like many international students, I experienced moments of uncertainty and self-doubt.

At the same time, it opened doors to incredible opportunities.

My doctoral research focused on Atomic Layer Deposition (ALD) of nickel oxide thin films for photoelectrochemical water splitting applications. The work involved developing ALD processes using novel precursors, understanding thin film growth mechanisms, and studying how deposition conditions influence material properties and device performance.

During this period, I gained extensive experience with thin-film synthesis and characterization techniques, including XPS, XRD, SEM, TEM, AFM, SIMS, optical, electrical, and photo-electrochemical measurements.

More importantly, I learned how research really works.

Scientific papers often present a clean and logical story. Reality is different. Research involves failed experiments, unexpected observations, countless troubleshooting sessions, and large amounts of perseverance.

Some of the most valuable lessons from my PhD came not from successful experiments but from the failures that forced me to think differently.

Joining imec

After completing my PhD, I joined imec in Belgium as an R&D Engineer.

For anyone working in semiconductors, imec is a very special place. It brings together scientists and engineers from around the world to tackle some of the biggest challenges in future electronics.

My work now focuses on advanced semiconductor technologies, where materials science, device physics, and process engineering intersect. Every day brings new opportunities to learn about gate stacks, dielectric materials, process integration, and nanoscale device engineering.

What excites me most is being part of a field that directly influences the technologies we use every day.

From smartphones and data centers to artificial intelligence and future computing systems, semiconductor innovation is shaping the modern world.
